1. Purpose

This policy sets out the principles by which the organisation protects the confidentiality, integrity and availability of the information it processes. It translates the requirements of ISO/IEC 27001, the applicable data protection legislation and the contractual obligations towards our customers into binding rules for everyday work. All subordinate standards, guidelines and procedures must be consistent with this policy.

2. Scope

The policy applies to all employees, temporary staff, contractors and service providers who have access to information or IT systems of the organisation, regardless of location and of whether the equipment is owned by the organisation. It covers information in every form, including paper documents, electronic files, e-mail, collaboration platforms and spoken communication.

3. Principles

Need to know. Access to information is granted only to the extent required for the person's role. Access rights are requested through the identity management process, approved by the information owner and reviewed at least every six months.

Classification. Every information asset is classified as public, internal, confidential or strictly confidential. The classification determines the permitted storage locations, transmission channels and retention periods. Where no classification is stated, information is treated as internal.

Secure by default. New systems and changes to existing systems are assessed for security risks before they go live. Default passwords are changed, unnecessary services are disabled and security updates are applied within the deadlines defined in the patch management standard.

Accountability. Security-relevant actions are logged in a way that allows them to be attributed to a person. Logs are protected against manipulation and are retained for at least twelve months.

All staffComply with this policy, complete the annual awareness training and report suspected incidents without delay.

5. Handling of security incidents

Any suspected security incident, such as a lost device, a phishing e-mail that has been opened, unusual system behaviour or the disclosure of information to unauthorised persons, must be reported immediately to the service desk or the security hotline. Reports may also be made anonymously. Staff must not investigate incidents on their own or delete evidence. The incident response team assesses each report, informs the data protection officer where personal data is affected and ensures that statutory notification deadlines are met.

6. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy require a documented risk assessment and the written approval of the Chief Information Security Officer. Exceptions are limited to a maximum of twelve months and are recorded in the exception register. Risks classified as high may only be accepted by the Executive Board.

7. Compliance and sanctions

Compliance with this policy is verified through internal audits, technical controls and the annual certification audit. Violations may result in disciplinary measures under employment law and, for contractors, in contractual consequences. Wilful or grossly negligent violations may also have consequences under civil or criminal law.

8. Review

This policy is reviewed at least once a year and whenever significant changes occur in the organisation, the threat landscape or the legal framework. Version 3 replaces version 2 of 14 September 2025; the changes concern the classification scheme (section 3) and the review interval for access rights.
